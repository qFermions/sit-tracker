# PRACTICE_SOURCES — source ledger for the practice-training content

*Every substantive traditional claim in the app's Learn tab traces to an entry
here. This file ships beside the app and is readable from Learn.*

**Verification method & honest limitation (2026-08-18):** this environment's
network proxy blocked direct fetches of every meditation-source domain, so each
source below was verified through search-engine-indexed content of the named
pages, not by loading the page itself. Attributions and instructional summaries
are high-confidence; character-exact wording was not verifiable, which is why
the app paraphrases with attribution instead of printing quotation marks.
Items that could not be grounded at all are excluded or marked UNVERIFIED.
No machine translation of Burmese was used anywhere.

## 1. MN 118 — Ānāpānasati Sutta (canonical baseline)

- **Text / translators:** Majjhima Nikāya 118; translations by Thanissaro
  Bhikkhu and Bhikkhu Sujato. Jhāna similes: AN 5.28, DN 2 (Thanissaro).
- **Tradition:** early Buddhist canon — the shared baseline, prior to all four
  modern systems below.
- **URLs:** https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN118.html ·
  https://suttacentral.net/mn118/en/sujato ·
  https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an05/an05.028.than.html
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-18 (via search index; direct fetch blocked).
- **Control:** dhammatalks.org teacher-controlled; suttacentral.net
  translator-controlled; accesstoinsight.org respected archive.
- **Informs:** Learn source map card 1; the "what MN 118 does NOT say" point.
- **Key verified points:** the 16-step scheme names no anatomical spot; the
  nostril/upper-lip anchor enters via the Abhidhamma Vibhaṅga gloss of
  *parimukhaṁ* ("nose tip / mouth sign") and the commentarial tradition —
  Thanissaro reads *parimukhaṁ* idiomatically ("to the fore"), Sujato as
  "in front"; translation genuinely contested. Step 3 is "sensitive to the
  entire body" (commentaries re-read this as the breath body — that re-reading
  is commentarial). Steps 5–6 train sensitivity to rapture and pleasure. The
  canonical jhāna similes describe whole-body pervasion of rapture/pleasure.
- **Caution:** attribute the nostril-spot instruction to the commentarial
  tradition and modern teachers, never to the sutta itself.

## 2. Pa-Auk Tawya Sayadaw — Mindfulness of Breathing; Knowing and Seeing

- **Tradition:** modern Burmese Theravāda, explicitly Visuddhimagga-based.
  One rigorous modern system — not "what Buddhism universally teaches."
- **URLs:** https://www.paauk.eu/images/meta/mindfulness-of-breathing_thumb.pdf
  (org-affiliated) · https://paauktawyausa.wordpress.com/dhamma/anapanassati/
  (org-affiliated) · free-distribution PDF mirrors (dhammatalks.net,
  dhammadownload.com, theravada.vn). Official paaukforestmonastery.org was
  unreachable from this environment.
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-18 (via search index).
- **Informs:** current-practice instruction (touching-point, natural breath,
  counting, waiting when the breath is faint); source map card 2; jhāna module;
  troubleshooting (faint breath, lights, wandering).
- **Key verified points:** attention at the touching point around the
  nostrils/upper lip, wherever contact is clearest; breath never controlled;
  never followed in/out (gatekeeper simile); counting until settled; as breath
  turns subtle, wait patiently at the spot — it is enough to know one is still
  breathing; nimitta terminology (parikamma / uggaha / paṭibhāga); early
  unstable nimittas are ignored, a stable counterpart sign eventually becomes
  the absorption object leading to jhāna under a teacher; pleasant feeling is
  not switched to as an object — the jhāna factors (incl. pīti/sukha) are
  discerned after absorption is developed; patience is the repeated watchword.
- **Caution:** Pa-Auk "jhāna" = deep fixed absorption on the counterpart
  nimitta (Visuddhimagga standard) — a different referent from Brasington's
  use of the same word. The reputed "I am not dead" reflection was UNVERIFIED
  and is not used in the app.

## 3. Leigh Brasington — leighb.com; Right Concentration (2015)

- **Tradition:** Western lay lineage (student of Ayya Khema), sutta-oriented.
  Everything labeled as Brasington's approach.
- **URLs:** https://www.leighb.com/accesscon.htm ·
  https://www.leighb.com/jhana3.htm · https://www.leighb.com/jhanas.htm ·
  reprints at buddhistinquiry.org, lionsroar.com, tricycle.org.
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-18 (via search index).
- **Informs:** source map card 3; jhāna module (the depth disagreement);
  troubleshooting (subtle breath as a milestone, lights ignored, striving).
- **Key verified points:** attention on breath sensation at the nostrils as
  the default access-concentration object; wandering → notice and return;
  signs of access concentration include background-wispy thoughts and a very
  subtle breath (it may seem to stop); then attention deliberately shifts from
  the breath to a pleasant (preferably physical) sensation, staying with its
  pleasantness until it grows into first jhāna — the growth happens by itself,
  and trying harder blocks it; stray lights/colors are to be ignored — even
  his "diffuse white light" nimitta is a road-sign, never the object; upright,
  alert, not-too-comfortable posture against sloth-and-torpor; he openly
  states his jhānas are lighter than Visuddhimagga absorption and argues the
  sutta jhānas are the practical referent.
- **Caution:** his sleepiness advice beyond alert-upright posture was
  UNVERIFIED and is not attributed in the app.

## 4. Thai Forest — Ajaan Lee Dhammadharo / Thanissaro Bhikkhu

- **Tradition:** Thai Forest kammaṭṭhāna lineage.
- **URLs:** https://www.dhammatalks.org/books/KeepingTheBreath/Section0009.html
  (Method 2) · https://www.dhammatalks.org/books/WithEachAndEveryBreath/Contents.html ·
  https://www.dhammatalks.org/books/InnerStrength/Section0014.html ·
  archive copy at accesstoinsight.org/lib/thai/lee/inmind.html.
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-18 (via search index).
- **Informs:** source map card 4; troubleshooting (wandering and striving via
  breath evaluation — the method channels effort into evaluating the breath's
  comfort; pleasant feeling spread with the breath; stilled breath allowed,
  never manufactured); jhāna module (whole-body character).
- **Key verified points:** Method 2 — long opening breaths; observe comfort
  and *adjust the breath until comfortable* (opposite of Pa-Auk's rule, though
  never forced); the nose tip is only one of several optional resting spots
  (mid-head, palate, throat, breastbone, navel); from step 5–6 awareness
  spreads through the whole body as breath energy; jhāna factors are mapped
  onto this whole-body work, echoing the canonical pervasion similes; when
  the breath grows still it is allowed to be still — never suppressed or held.
- **Caution:** "breath" here means whole-body breath energy, not only nostril
  airflow — the app must not equate the two. Thanissaro's traditional
  reassurance that a stilled breath is fine because oxygen "comes in through
  the pores" is physiologically false and is NOT repeated by the app; the app
  keeps only the sound intent (passive stillness, never suppression).
  His specific visions-chapter wording: UNVERIFIED (section existence
  verified via contents page).

## Safety line (all sources)

No source instructs breath-holding, restriction, or hyperventilation; Pa-Auk
and Brasington forbid controlling the breath entirely, Ajaan Lee permits only
comfort-seeking adjustment without force. The app states: stillness of breath
is allowed to happen, never made to happen; never hold the breath.
