The daily sit script
  1. Sit erect, eyes closed. Three slow breaths to settle.
  2. Place attention where the breath touches — nostril rim or upper lip. One spot. Don't follow the breath inward or outward (the classical image: a gatekeeper checks people at the gate, he doesn't walk with them).
  3. Know the breath as just in / out. Long as long, short as short. Don't control it.
  4. Count: in-out "one" … in-out "eight", restart. Count after each breath ends. Drop counting once the mind stays settled 15–20 minutes without it.
  5. Then know the beginning, middle, and end of each breath at the touch point.
  6. When the breath becomes faint, do not breathe harder to find it. Keep attention on the spot and wait — calmness makes it reappear.

Compressed from Pa-Auk Sayadaw, “Knowing and Seeing”, ch. 1 — the full text is in the source library below.

Tuning energy on every sit

The canon's master dial (SN 51.20): energy not too slack, not too tense; not constricted inward (drifting toward dullness), not scattered outward (drifting toward distraction). Check it once when you sit down and once mid-sit.

If a light appears, if the breath fades, if a pleasant feeling arises — see Troubleshooting below: the traditions answer differently, and the differences are kept.

What breath meditation reaches, and what it doesn't

The tradition's own answer: breath meditation carries a practitioner to deep absorption (the four jhānas) and — combined with insight practice — to liberation, which the texts treat as the point of the whole path. The five “powers” described in scripture require switching to different meditation objects (kasinas) after absorption is mastered. Breath is the engine; kasinas are described as the steering wheel.

Commitment, realistically

The sources are blunt about dose: the early stages ask for daily sitting and eventually retreat time; the deep stages are described as a renunciant's career, not a hobby. The insight track is explicitly compatible with ordinary life. Every source agrees on the floor: ethical conduct, one method at a time, and a qualified teacher once concentration deepens.

Data

Everything lives in this browser's localStorage (key jhanaTracker.v2). Browser data can be cleared by the OS or the user — export regularly. Data is not encrypted and never leaves this device unless you configure an AI provider or export it yourself.

AI provider (optional)

By default the Journal uses a local, deterministic parser — nothing leaves the device. If you configure a provider, journal text is sent to that endpoint when you press “Create draft”. The key is stored only in this browser's localStorage, in plain text. What that provider stores, logs, or trains on is governed by their terms, not by this app — check them before sending practice notes you would not want retained.

If the provider fails or returns malformed output, the app falls back to the local parser automatically.
